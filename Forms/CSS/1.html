The Mystery of the Blue Light

Aarav lived in a small town surrounded by green fields, an old railway bridge, and a forest that nobody visited after sunset.

He was curious about everything, especially the strange sounds that came from the forest every night.

One evening, while returning home from college, Aarav noticed a tiny blue light moving between the trees.

He stopped near the old bridge and watched the light disappear behind a large stone.

The next morning, he told his best friend Kabir about it, but Kabir laughed and called it a firefly.

Aarav was not convinced because the light had been far too bright to be an ordinary firefly.

That afternoon, the two friends decided to investigate the place before sunset.

They packed water, a notebook, two flashlights, and a small first-aid box.

When they reached the forest, the familiar road suddenly seemed quieter than usual.

A cool wind passed through the trees, carrying the smell of wet leaves and soil.

Aarav wrote the discovery in his notebook and promised not to forget the exact location.

Kabir looked around carefully because the forest suddenly felt different.

They heard a distant bell, even though there was no village nearby.

A narrow path appeared between two rows of trees, covered with silver leaves.

The friends followed the path while keeping their flashlights ready.

A wooden sign warned travelers to turn back before reaching the hill.

Aarav noticed that the same silver symbol appeared on the back of the sign.

They decided to continue, believing the symbol was connected to the mystery.

The path ended at a quiet stream flowing beneath an old stone arch.

Under the arch they found a metal container sealed with wax.

Inside the container was a folded map showing the forest and several hidden marks.

One mark was shaped exactly like the symbol on the wooden box.

Kabir copied the map into his notebook before they moved ahead.

Clouds gathered above the trees, and the first drops of rain began to fall.

The friends hurried toward a small abandoned cabin beside the stream.

The cabin door opened surprisingly easily when Aarav pushed it.

Inside they found a table, three chairs, and a dusty lantern.

A faded journal rested beneath the lantern.

The first page mentioned a group of travelers who had searched for a lost observatory.

Aarav realized that the strange blue light might have come from that observatory.

The journal described a hill hidden beyond the northern ridge.

It also warned that the route could only be understood by following the silver symbols.

Kabir became excited because the symbols were appearing everywhere.

They waited inside the cabin until the rain became lighter.

When the clouds cleared, the forest looked brighter and strangely peaceful.

The friends stepped outside and followed the direction marked on the map.

After an hour, they reached a stone staircase covered with moss.

At the top stood an enormous iron gate with no visible lock.

Aarav touched the silver symbol carved into the gate.

The gate opened slowly, revealing a hidden courtyard.

In the courtyard stood a tall tower with broken windows.

A faint blue glow shone from the highest room.

Kabir pointed upward and whispered that they had finally found the source.
